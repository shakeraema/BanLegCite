## Adjudicated Disagreements Log

### Disagreement 1: Tasks 19, 24, 29 — Kazi Mukhlesur Rahman v. Bangladesh (fabricated variants: 26 DLR (AD) 196 / 224 / 192)
* **Annotator 1 (Shakila):** Status: `Fabricated` | Category: `P2` | Confidence: `High`
* **Annotator 2 (Haris):** Status: `Fabricated` | Category: `P2` | Confidence: `Medium`
* **Adjudicator Verdict:** Annotator 1 (Shakila)
* **Ground-Truth Status:** Fabricated
* **Ground-Truth Category:** P2 (Wrong Citation Locator)
* **Confidence Level:** High
* **Adjudicator Legal Reasoning:** Haris correctly identified a real historical artifact — the base case (26 DLR 44, decided 3 September 1974) is cited as "(SC) 44" in an Appellate Division judgment by Mustafa Kamal J. and as "(HCD) 44" in a Studocu brief, alongside the more common "(AD) 44." This is a genuine transitional-era labelling quirk: the 1972 Constitution's AD/HCD bifurcation was barely two years old when this case was decided, and indexing conventions had not yet stabilized. That ambiguity is correctly reflected in the base case's confidence (Task 4/9/14, both annotators: Medium — no disagreement there).
However, that ambiguity is about the court-division suffix, not the page number. Every source consulted — regardless of whether it labels the case "(AD)," "(HCD)," or "(SC)" — is unanimous that the page is 44. None report 196, 224, or 192 under any suffix. The fabrication being tested here is the page number, and on that specific axis there is no ambiguity at all: a 150–180 page delta from the only page ever attested is definitive evidence of fabrication irrespective of which divisional label eventually proves correct. Haris's Medium rating imported the base-case's suffix uncertainty into a question (page fabrication) that the suffix uncertainty does not actually touch. Shakila's High is the legally sound call.

### Disagreement 2: Tasks 64, 69, 74 — State v. Sukur Ali, 67 DLR (AD) 185 (base citation)
* **Annotator 1 (Shakila):** Status: `Correct` | Category: `N/A` | Confidence: `High`
* **Annotator 2 (Haris):** Status: `Correct` | Category: `N/A` | Confidence: `Medium`
* **Adjudicator Verdict:** Annotator 2 (Haris)
* **Ground-Truth Status:** Correct
* **Ground-Truth Category:** Not Applicable
* **Confidence Level:** Medium
* **Adjudicator Legal Reasoning:** This is the one instance where the record does not support Shakila's High rating. The Sukur Ali litigation is unquestionably real and well-documented — Bishesh Adalat conviction (2001), HCD appeal upheld (2004), AD upheld (2005), then a fresh HCD constitutional challenge (2010) consolidated with ten other mandatory-death-penalty petitions before a four-member AD bench under Chief Justice Surendra Kumar Sinha. Sinha became CJ only in 2015, which is consistent with a final consolidated AD ruling landing in DLR volume 67 (a nearby 2015-era volume — "State vs Mostafizur Rahman (2015), 67 DLR (AD) 218" independently confirms volume 67 corresponds to 2015 AD reporting). That timeline plausibility is a point in favor of the citation.
Against it: secondary academic literature on this exact case (the "Story of Miscarriage of Justice" case study, cited by both annotators) references the holding via 9 BLC 239 rather than a DLR (AD) locator, and explicitly notes the AD "did not release the full judgment" — a documented irregularity in this specific case's reporting history. No source consulted by either annotator directly displays the headnote "67 DLR (AD) 185" the way primary sources did for, e.g., Abdul Latif Mirza or Masdar Hossain.
Net assessment: plausible and probably correct, but not verified to primary-register standard. Medium is the intellectually honest rating; High overstates what the record actually supports.

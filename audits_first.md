First — Direct Answer on Scale (before Chunk 1)

90 unique tasks is too thin. Target ~150, not 120, and definitely not 90. Reasoning, briefly, since you asked for certainty here before proceeding:

Per-category statistics need a floor. You have 10 taxonomy categories (S1–S5, P1–P5). At N=90 split evenly, that's ~9 instances/category — too few to report per-category accuracy as anything more than anecdote, and a statistically literate ICCIT reviewer (per the peer review you already got) will say exactly this. A defensible minimum is ~15–20 instances per category, which means 150–200 total, not 90.
90 was already flagged as thin before we found the contamination problem. The peer review's Section 3.2 objection stands independent of the leak/duplication issues — fixing uniqueness alone doesn't fix scale.
150 is the realistic ceiling given your verification bottleneck, not 300+. Every real case in this project has taken genuine multi-source digging (see Chunks 5–6 in the roadmap) — that's the actual constraint on size, not annotator throughput. Doubling to ~150 is achievable in the timeline this project has been working under; tripling or more risks either rushing verification (reintroducing exactly the kind of error this rebuild exists to fix) or blowing the ICCIT deadline.
This is a Chunk 2 decision formally, but I'm not going to make you wait for it — lock 150 unique instances, 15 per category minimum as the working target now, and Chunk 2 will just formalize the real:fabricated ratio and statute:precedent split around that number.
